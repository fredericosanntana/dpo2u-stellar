Task 3 The date

30 Oct
2026

Six weeks away

Brazilian institutions stop being allowed to transact with unauthorised counterparties.

From that date, financial and payment institutions may not carry out or enable virtual asset operations with counterparties that provide virtual asset services and are not authorised by the Banco Central — or in the authorisation process.

It is also the authorisation deadline for providers already operating. Two populations, the same date, opposite sides: providers must be authorised, banks must verify that they are.

Task 3 The calendar

Institutions are inside the window, not approaching it.

Annually
31 Mar AML effectiveness report — Circular BCB 3.978, arts. 62–65. Base date 31 December. Binding since 2020.
2 Feb 2026 Resolutions BCB 519 / 520 / 521 in force. Travel rule phase I (domestic) begins — running now.
30 Oct 2026 Authorisation deadline, and the prohibition on unauthorised counterparties begins.
2 Feb 2027 Travel rule phase II — extends to cross-border transfers.
2 Feb 2028 Travel rule full compliance.

Across both travel-rule phases the norm permits a documented customer self-declaration to identify the parties. It is the weakest control available, and it governs non-custodial wallet transfers throughout. Our counterparty rule returns review on that branch — permitted, but never silently.

Task 3 Licensing

The barrier is not the licence. It is the structure.

An institution already authorised by the Central Bank does not need a new licence to provide intermediation or custody of virtual assets. It must:

— Notify the BCB at least 90 days in advance;

— Adapt its policies and structures to the new regime.

This is the point most often misstated, including by vendors. For an incumbent bank the obstacle is demonstrating adapted controls, on a calendar that has already started — which is what we sell.

SPSAV · three modalities

Intermediária — intermediation of trading and distribution
Custodiante — custody and control of assets
Corretora — both of the above

Mandatory segregation between customer funds and virtual assets and the institution's own. Directors resident in Brazil. Stablecoin issuers must additionally prove reserves on a continuing basis.

Task 3 The obligation nobody cites

Brazil has required demonstrated effectiveness since 2020.

Circular BCB 3.978, articles 62 to 65: an annual report on the effectiveness of AML controls — not on their existence.

— Methodology

— Tests applied

— Qualification of the assessor

— Deficiencies, and an action plan

Base date 31 December; submitted by 31 March.

A signed, hash-linked trail of every evaluation is a record of tests applied.

The report is what the protocol emits as a by-product, not as a feature. We are not adding an obligation — we are sealing one that already exists and is assembled by hand today.

FATF calls the move from documented policy to demonstrated effectiveness a direction of travel. In Brazil it has been dated law for six years.

Task 3 Why this cannot be bought off a shelf

The threshold is a parameter the norm refuses to fix.

Circular BCB 3.978 does not set a beneficial-ownership threshold. Each institution sets its own on a risk basis, capped at 25%, justified and documented — and a representative exercising actual control counts regardless of percentage.

The ceiling itself moves across Latin America: 5% in Colombia, 10% in Argentina, 15% in Uruguay, 25% in Brazil. A threshold compiled in as a constant satisfies the norm in none of them. This is why law has to become code rather than configuration.

brazilUboParams({
  institutionThresholdPct: 30,
  riskBasisRef: 'ARC-2026-004',
});

// the configuration does not build:
RangeError: institutionThresholdPct 30%
  exceeds the jurisdiction cap of 25%

An illegal configuration is not a warning to triage later. The policy set cannot be constructed.

The same rule at 10% and at 25% has a different identity, so the attestation records which rule was in force — not merely that a rule by that name existed.

The question the DPO asks first

Chain immutability versus the right to erasure.

Only a hash and a commitment are anchored. Erasing the evidence does not break the seal, because the seal never held the data. The attestation still proves an authorised issuer reached a verdict under a stated policy at a stated time.

A property of the design, not a mitigation applied to it. This is the answer that decides whether a DPO signs off or escalates.

Task 4 On Midnight

The network fits the product, not the other way round.

Our hard constraint is that customer evidence never leaves the perimeter. Midnight generates proofs on a local proof server, on the user's own machine — our requirement written into the network's architecture.

On a transparent chain, publishing the verdict means publishing the score.

Stated plainly: the proof does not establish that the score is true — an authorised issuer could witness a false number. What binds it to reality is issuer authorisation, the score commitment, and the signed trail inside the institution.
